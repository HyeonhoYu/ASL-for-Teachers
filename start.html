What is ASL?

American Sign Language is a complete, natural language used by Deaf communities in the United States and parts of Canada. It has its own grammar and word order, and it uses handshape, movement, location, facial expression, and the space around the signer to make meaning.

ASL is not English on the hands, and it is not the same around the world. Learning a few signs is a start, and it is also a way to show respect for a language and a culture.

How to use this site

Learn takes you through four modules in order. Practice has classroom scenarios, a quiz, and Mirror Practice with your webcam. The Sign Dictionary lets you look up any sign on the site.

On every sign page you can slow the video down, loop it, and mirror it so the signer's hands match yours. Mark a sign as learned to track your progress. Progress is saved in this browser only.

A note on interpreters

This site helps you communicate basic classroom messages, build a welcoming classroom, and understand Deaf culture. It does not prepare you to interpret.

Deaf and hard of hearing students have the right to full communication access. When a student needs an interpreter, that must be a qualified professional. Your role is to teach, to plan with the interpreter, and to make your classroom visually accessible.
